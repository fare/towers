### The Towers Approach

* Decompose human and machine activities in levels of abstraction.

* View, discuss and fix each issue at the most meaningful level.

* Maintain semantic alignment between levels with formal methods.

* Navigate levels not just during design, but during execution.

* All the way from informal management to automated implementation.

### Increase AI (and Human) Safety

* Explainable Proofs -- Formalize Each Property at its Level

* Semantic Grounding -- Prevent Hallucinations

* Airtight Verification -- Prevent Semantic Leaks Between Levels

* Debugging Enabled -- Analyze Each Issue At The Right Level

* Complexity Managed -- Avoid Combinatorial Explosion

## Levels of Discourse

### Discourse at too low-level

* Wanting to talk about a tree, must do it in terms of cells.

* Drowning in details.

* Expensive: more words or tokens to say anything.

* Make insight as to what is happening harder.

* The mind boggles.

### Discourse at too high-level

* Wanting to talk about a tree, must do it in terms of forest.

* Can't even see the details you care about.

* Inexpressive: Too abstract to matter.

* Make insight as to what is happening impossible.

* The mind is blinded.

### Discourse at the right level

* Wanting to talk about a tree, talk about the tree.

* Speak exactly of the details that matter, neither too high nor too low.

* Cheap and Expressive: interact with levels below and above.

* The right insight for justifying the decision to take.

* The mind (Human or AI) is maximally enabled.

### Adjustable Discourse Level

* Each level allows formal reasoning

* Maintain mapping between levels

* Observability: Climb levels up *at runtime*

* Dynamically Adjust to the Right Level

* Integrates human, AI and Algorithmic processes

### Continuous Debuggability with Observability

* Consistently Dive into the System and Back

* Each Level, Each Link Explainable

* Safely Refactor Automated Agent Hierarchies

* Dynamic Evolution vs Static Command-and-Control

* Adaptability to Context

## Application to Entire Organizations

### Application to Hierarchical Human Organizations

* Managers divide their task in parts, each with its subgoal

* Each part delegated to subordinates who refine and execute

* Each layer implements higher-level goals, specifies lower-level execution

* “Implementation” requirements are Semantic Alignment

* “Observability” enables deep dive, diagnosis, continuous improvement

### Application to Hierarchical AI Agents: Same, but...

* Humans: Expensive social/economic/legal enforcement.

* AIs: Cheap alignment through training, verification, selection.

* Humans: Deeper, Tighter Real-World Feedback.

* AIs: can afford deeper, tighter formal verification.

* AIs adapt faster—also diverge faster, especially at scale.

### Application to Algorithmic Agents: samer

* Algorithms: systematically correct or incorrect, vulnerable

* Algorithms: alignment through careful specification, design

* Algorithms: static, no feedback—until externally upgraded

* Algorithms: at scale, *require* formal verification

* Algorithms: no adaptation, no divergence, scales most of all

### Same Implementations Across Formalisms

* Managerial hierarchies and PKIs

* Gantt charts, Petri nets, ...

* Human, AI, Robotic or Algorithmic workflows.

* Formal Specifications, logical models

* Executable code, physical models
